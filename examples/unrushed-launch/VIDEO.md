# VIDEO

## The piece
- **Topic**: Unrushed 0.3 now directs an editor's library of effects, zooms, transitions and sound.
- **The one sentence the viewer leaves with**: Unrushed decides which effects a cut needs, and which it doesn't.
- **Audience**: creators and product designers who edit with AI tools such as ChatCut; they have seen the "AI edit" look and dislike it.
- **Platform**: LinkedIn and X
- **Format**: 1920×1080, 24 fps
- **Duration**: 39 s
- **Sound**: voice, with a quiet music bed

## The one true thing
- **Problem**: auto-edits pile zooms, shakes and flashes on every cut, and the talk gets harder to follow.
- **Emotion**: relief.
- **The one real feature this piece is for**: the cue sheet, where every effect needs a reason before it is applied.

## Brand inventory
- **Logo shape**: the `>` symbol made of dots.
- **Core control**: the command line (`> unrushed critique`).
- **Material**: LED dots on paper.
- **Key verb**: hold.
- **Success moment**: the detector prints no flags.
- **Voice**: precise, calm, critical; "beat", "cut", "hold".
- **What it would never do**: shake, flash, bounce, or say "epic".

## Goal
- **Type of piece**: launch
- **Goal**: launch
- **What the viewer already knows or believes**: auto-edits add zooms and shakes everywhere.
- **What they should feel or do afterwards**: relief; then install the skill.

## Spine
- **Hook**: the same talking head, edited "by AI": four punch-ins and a flash in five seconds. Freeze. `> unrushed critique`.
- **Promise**: "Your editor's library has 50 effects. Your cut needs about three."
- **Proof**:
  1. the intent table chooses a Magnifying Glass to explain a UI detail;
  2. the cue sheet ties each zoom to a word;
  3. the detector flags the shake.
- **Payoff**: the same cut, re-edited with three cues; the zoom lands exactly on "never".
- **Ask**: `> npx skills add unrushed`, readable, final frame held 1.2 s. The GitHub URL goes in the post copy.

## Through-line
- **Chosen**: one orange dot that marks "the focus": it jumps around too much in the hook, and settles on one word in the payoff.
- **Why**: it is the brand's accent rule, and it makes the claim visible: one focus at a time.
- **Cover-the-logo test**: partly. Any editing tool could use a dot as "the focus"; what ties it to this brand is that the symbol is built from the same dots and the brand allows one accent per frame. The dot assembling the symbol at the end is close to the Never ship pattern "a dot that becomes the logo": it is kept only because the dot carried the argument all the way through.
- **Alternatives considered**: a frame ruler that fills with cues; a library panel that empties.
